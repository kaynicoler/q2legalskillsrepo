Balanced NDA Example — Low Risk

Shows what a well-balanced NDA looks like and how to report acceptable confidentiality terms without creating unnecessary redlines.

Prompt

I'm reviewing this NDA for my company. Review it for confidentiality risk and identify anything that needs to be negotiated.

Risk Level: Low

Executive Summary

Well-balanced mutual NDA with commercially reasonable protections for both parties. The confidentiality obligations, exclusions, permitted disclosures, duration, and remedies are generally consistent with market practice.

No critical issues identified. A few minor drafting points could be improved, but none warrant delaying execution unless the underlying transaction involves unusually sensitive information.

Key Terms

- NDA Structure: Mutual
- Confidentiality Period: 3 years
- Trade Secrets: Protected while qualifying as trade secrets
- Use Restriction: Evaluation of proposed business relationship only
- Permitted Recipients: Employees, affiliates, advisors, and contractors with need to know
- Required Disclosure: Permitted with notice where legally allowed
- Return / Destruction: Upon request, subject to standard archival exceptions
- Residuals: None
- Governing Law: Delaware

Risk Analysis

Minor — Definition of Representatives

Example language:
“Representatives include a party's employees, officers, directors, affiliates, professional advisors, and contractors who have a need to know the Confidential Information.”

Issue:
The receiving party remains responsible for breaches by its Representatives.

Assessment:
This is customary and reasonable because disclosure is limited to persons with a need to know who are subject to confidentiality obligations.

Optional ask:
Limit responsibility to Representatives that the receiving party actually authorizes to receive Confidential Information.

Minor — Return and Destruction

Example language:
“Upon request, the Receiving Party will return or destroy Confidential Information, except for copies maintained through routine backup, legal, regulatory, or document-retention systems.”

Issue:
Archival copies may remain after a destruction request.

Assessment:
Standard and operationally necessary. Retained copies remain subject to the NDA.

Optional ask:
Clarify expressly that retained archival copies remain protected for the applicable confidentiality period.

Reviewed & Acceptable

- Mutuality: Acceptable — obligations apply equally to both parties
- Confidential Information Definition: Acceptable — broad enough to protect legitimate confidential information without being unlimited
- Confidentiality Exclusions: Acceptable — includes prior knowledge, public information, independent development, and lawful third-party receipt
- Purpose / Use Restriction: Acceptable — information may be used only to evaluate or pursue the contemplated relationship
- Representatives: Acceptable — disclosure limited to persons with a need to know and confidentiality obligations
- Responsibility for Representatives: Acceptable — receiving party is responsible for authorized recipients
- Compelled Disclosure: Acceptable — disclosure permitted when legally required, with notice where permitted
- Confidentiality Duration: Acceptable — three-year protection period is commercially reasonable
- Trade Secrets: Acceptable — trade secrets remain protected while legally qualifying as trade secrets
- Return / Destruction: Acceptable — standard return/destruction requirement with reasonable archival exceptions
- Ownership: Acceptable — disclosure does not transfer ownership of confidential information
- No License: Acceptable — no implied IP license created by disclosure
- No Obligation to Proceed: Acceptable — neither party is required to enter a transaction
- No Warranty: Acceptable — confidential information provided without representations as to completeness or accuracy

- Residuals: Acceptable — no residual-knowledge provision
- Publicity: Acceptable — no right to use the other party's name or relationship without consent
- Assignment: Acceptable — reasonable restrictions with standard successor / change-of-control treatment

Overall Recommendation

Approve as drafted.

The NDA provides reciprocal protections and does not materially favor either party. The identified issues are minor and do not justify spending significant negotiation capital absent unusual sensitivity or deal-specific concerns.

No redlines are required for an ordinary commercial evaluation.

Key Lesson

Not every NDA requires redlines. When confidentiality terms are balanced:

1. Confirm what works — identify provisions that have been substantively reviewed and are acceptable.
2. Distinguish risk from preference — a provision should not be flagged merely because alternative language could be drafted.
3. Consider materiality — focus negotiation on provisions that create meaningful legal or operational exposure.
4. Preserve negotiation capital — avoid unnecessary edits to customary provisions.
5. Evaluate the NDA as a whole — mutuality, reasonable scope, workable confidentiality periods, standard exclusions, and practical disclosure rules generally indicate a balanced agreement.

Signs of a Balanced NDA

- Mutual confidentiality obligations.
- Confidential Information is defined clearly without making essentially everything confidential forever.
- Standard exclusions for information that is public, previously known, independently developed, or lawfully obtained elsewhere.
- Disclosure to employees, affiliates, contractors, and professional advisors on a need-to-know basis.
- Permitted disclosure when required by law or legal process.
- Reasonable notice obligations for compelled disclosure.
- A defined confidentiality period for ordinary business information.
- Continued protection for qualifying trade secrets.
- Practical return-and-destruction obligations with backup and legal-retention exceptions.
- No residual-knowledge clause allowing unrestricted use of remembered information.
- No unexpected transfer or license of intellectual property.
- Equitable remedies that are reciprocal and do not create disproportionate liability.
- No hidden non-compete, non-solicit, exclusivity, standstill, or other substantive business restrictions.
- Neither party receives dramatically broader rights or materially heavier obligations than the other.
