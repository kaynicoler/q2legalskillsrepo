# Q2 NDA Standard Positions
## Extracted from Q2 NDA Templates
 
Six Q2 NDA variants exist. Select the correct one based on counterparty jurisdiction and
deal structure. All variants share the same core terms — differences are noted per section.
 
---
 
## Template Selection Guide
 
| Situation | Template to Use |
|---|---|
| Standard two-party deal, US counterparty | Standard |
| Three parties involved (e.g. Q2 + Partner + Client) | Three-Party |
| Three parties, one is an existing Q2 customer | Three-Party (Existing Customer Language) |
| Counterparty is UK-based | UK Law |
| Counterparty is Australia-based | Australia Law |
| Counterparty is India-based | India Law |
 
**When reviewing a counterparty NDA:** Compare against the Standard template. If the counterparty
is international, also check for the applicable jurisdiction variant and note where governing law
deviates from Q2's standard.
 
---
 
## 1. Agreement Type
 
**Q2 Standard:** Mutual Confidentiality Agreement (bilateral — both parties disclose and receive)
 
**Three-Party variants:** "Confidentiality Agreement" (not labeled "Mutual" — all three parties
disclose to and receive from each other)
 
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🔴 One-way NDA where only Q2 is bound as Recipient (Q2 should always have mutual obligations)
- 🟡 "Unilateral" or "one-way" framing without discussion — confirm intent with business team
---
 
## 2. Confidential Information Definition
 
**Q2 Standard (consistent across all variants):**
 
CI includes ALL of the following (no marking requirement for most categories):
- (a) Financial records, business data, methodology, sales/marketing strategies, pricing, customer/supplier lists, product/service info, designs, schematics, formulas, technical info, inventions, trade secrets, software (including source code), documentation, specs, third-party CI incorporated in disclosures, and all copies — **regardless of whether marked confidential**
- (b) Information marked "Confidential" or "Proprietary"
- (c) Information that by its nature a reasonable person would consider confidential
- (d) Oral/demonstration disclosures identified as confidential at time of disclosure
**Exclusions (standard — acceptable in counterparty paper):**
1. In public domain at time of disclosure (not through Recipient's fault)
2. Rightfully in Recipient's possession free of confidence obligation
3. Independently developed without reference to CI
**Note — Q2 NDA exclusions are narrower than MPA exclusions:** The NDA omits the MPA's
"required by law" exclusion from the definition itself (it appears instead in the obligations
section as a permitted disclosure with notice). Also omits the MPA's "prior written approval"
exclusion. This is intentional — NDAs are meant to be tighter.
 
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🔴 Marking requirement — CI must be stamped "Confidential" to qualify (Q2 standard has no such requirement for most categories)
- 🔴 Excluding oral disclosures entirely
- 🟡 Adding a "residuals" clause (right to use CI retained in unaided memory — common in tech NDAs but Q2 does not include this)
- 🟡 Narrowing definition to exclude pricing, customer lists, or product roadmaps
---
 
## 3. Confidentiality Obligations
 
**Q2 Standard:**
- Use CI **solely for the Purpose** (considering/pursuing a mutually beneficial business relationship)
- Standard of care: same as Recipient's own comparable information, **no less than reasonable care**
- Disclosure permitted only to employees (including wholly-owned subsidiary employees, parent company employees, other wholly-owned subsidiary employees), agents, or consultants who:
  - Must be directly involved with CI for the Purpose
  - Are bound by confidentiality terms substantially similar to this Agreement
- **No third-party disclosure** (including consultants/subcontractors) without Discloser's **specific written approval**
- **Exception:** Attorneys and financial advisors bound by professional confidentiality obligations (no written agreement required — professional obligation sufficient)
- Prohibited: reverse engineering, decompiling, analyzing for structure/composition, disassembly of any CI
- **Immediate notice** to Discloser of any known unauthorized use or disclosure
- Reasonable assistance to Discloser in remedying unauthorized use/disclosure
- Permitted legal disclosure: valid court order or law — with **prompt prior written notice** to Discloser (unless prohibited by law) and reasonable cooperation for protective order
**Three-Party (Existing Customer) variant — Purpose language:**
The Purpose field is left as `<<PURPOSE BASED ON REQUEST CONTEXT>>` — must be tailored to the
specific deal. Flag if it is left blank or is too broad (e.g., "any business purpose").
 
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🔴 Broader permitted purpose than "considering and pursuing a mutually beneficial business relationship" — flag any open-ended purpose
- 🔴 No prohibition on reverse engineering
- 🔴 No notice obligation for unauthorized disclosures
- 🔴 Omitting the requirement that employees/consultants be bound by written confidentiality terms
- 🟡 Allowing disclosure to "affiliates" broadly without limiting to those with need to know
- 🟡 No cooperation obligation for protective orders when legal disclosure is required
- 🟡 Legal disclosure carve-out without notice requirement
---
 
## 4. Ownership
 
**Q2 Standard:**
- All CI and materials furnished by Discloser **remain the property of Discloser or its licensors**
- Recipient **does not acquire any ownership interest** in CI, including through combination with Recipient's own CI
- No license grant implied by disclosure
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🔴 Any ownership claim over CI disclosed by Q2
- 🔴 Joint ownership of analyses or derivatives created using Q2's CI
- 🟡 Missing explicit acknowledgment that no IP license is granted
---
 
## 5. Term
 
**Q2 Standard (consistent across all variants):**
- Agreement term: **2 years** from Effective Date
- Early termination: either party on **90 days' prior written notice**
- Alternative termination: parties execute a superseding agreement governing CI use
**Survival of obligations post-termination/expiration:**
- General CI obligations survive for **5 years** after expiration or termination
- **Trade secrets and pricing methodologies: perpetual obligations** (no time limit)
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🔴 Survival period shorter than 3 years (Q2 standard is 5 years)
- 🔴 No perpetual obligation for trade secrets
- 🟡 Agreement term longer than 3 years without Q2 consent (creates long-term CI obligations)
- 🟡 Survival period tied to "term" rather than running from expiration/termination
---
 
## 6. Return / Destruction of Confidential Information
 
**Q2 Standard:**
- Trigger: **Discloser's written request** (not automatic on termination — on-demand)
- Timing: within **10 business days** of request
- Obligation: securely dispose of OR deliver all Discloser materials and CI in Recipient's possession or control
- Written certification: provided **if requested** by Discloser
- **IT backup exception:** Recipient may retain CI maintained as archive copies on disaster recovery/IT backup systems; such copies destroyed on normal expiration of backup files; CI in backup remains subject to NDA obligations
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🔴 No return/destruction obligation
- 🔴 Return window longer than 30 days
- 🔴 Broad retention rights beyond the narrow IT backup exception
- 🟡 No certification obligation
- 🟡 Automatic destruction without option to return (Q2 standard preserves both options)
---
 
## 7. Governing Law
 
**Q2 Standard (two-party US / Three-Party):**
- Laws of the **United States of America and the State of Texas**, exclusive of conflict of laws
- Prevailing party entitled to attorneys' fees, expert witness fees, and out-of-pocket costs
**UK variant:** English Law (exclusive of conflict of laws)
**Australia variant:** Laws of New South Wales, Australia (exclusive of conflict of laws)
**India variant:** Laws of India (exclusive of conflict of laws)
 
**All variants include prevailing party attorneys' fees** — this is a Q2 standard position.
 
**Flag if counterparty proposes (on a US deal):**
- 🔴 Non-Texas governing law (Delaware, California, New York, etc.)
- 🔴 No attorneys' fees provision for prevailing party
- 🟡 Mandatory arbitration (Q2 NDA templates do not include arbitration)
- 🟡 Exclusive jurisdiction outside Texas for a US counterparty
---
 
## 8. Assignment
 
**Q2 Standard:** Neither party may assign without prior written consent of the other.
Three-Party variants: No party may assign without prior written consent of **all other parties**.
 
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🟡 Unilateral assignment right (one party can assign without consent)
- 🟡 Assignment permitted in M&A without consent (Q2 NDA is stricter than MPA on this — no M&A exception)
---
 
## 9. Injunctive Relief
 
**Q2 Standard:** Recipient acknowledges Discloser would be irreparably damaged by breach; Discloser
may seek immediate injunctive relief in addition to any other remedy at law or in equity.
 
**Flag if counterparty proposes:**
- 🟡 Removing injunctive relief provision or requiring bond posting before injunction
---
 
## 10. Three-Party / Existing Customer Specific Provisions
 
**Three-Party (Existing Customer Language) — § 13 additional clause:**
- This NDA does **not supersede, terminate, or amend** any Existing Customer Agreement between Q2
  and any other party (master agreements, subscription agreements, SOWs, order forms)
- Existing Customer Agreements remain in full force
- In case of conflict: Existing Customer Agreement controls between parties to that agreement
**Purpose field:** Must be tailored — `<<PURPOSE BASED ON REQUEST CONTEXT>>` is a placeholder.
Always confirm the Purpose is specific and limited before the NDA is executed.
 
**Flag in Three-Party / Existing Customer reviews:**
- 🔴 Purpose field left blank or as placeholder
- 🔴 NDA language that purports to supersede an existing Q2 customer agreement
- 🟡 Existing Customer conflict clause is missing (use this variant when an existing agreement exists)
---
 
## 11. What Q2 NDAs Do NOT Include (flag if counterparty adds these)
 
The following provisions do **not** appear in any Q2 NDA template and should be flagged:
 
| Provision | Risk / Q2 Position |
|---|---|
| Residuals clause | Allows CI retained in memory to be freely used — 🔴 reject |
| Non-solicitation of employees | Outside NDA scope — 🟡 note, may need separate agreement |
| Non-compete | Outside NDA scope — 🔴 reject in an NDA context |
| Limitation of liability clause | NDAs should not cap liability for CI breach — 🔴 reject |
| Arbitration clause | Q2 NDA templates use courts only — 🟡 flag |
| Standstill provision | May be appropriate in M&A context — escalate to GC |
| Exclusivity provision | Outside NDA scope — 🟡 escalate |
