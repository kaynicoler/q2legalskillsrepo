# Q2 NDA Review Playbook
 
**Category:** General
**Owner:** Q2 Legal
**Purpose:** Standard positions, acceptable deviations, preferred revisions, and counterparty-facing comments for first-pass NDA review.
 
---
 
## How to Use This Playbook
 
Review the actual NDA text first. Do not infer missing language from templates, file names, headings, prior agreements, metadata, or assumptions.
 
Apply sources in this order:
 
1. NDA under review
2. Q2 NDA Review Playbook
3. Q2 standard NDA templates, as fallback drafting support only
4. Other uploaded guidance
For each clause:
 
1. Identify whether the clause is **Present**, **Not found**, or **Unclear**.
2. Quote or closely paraphrase only the actual NDA language supporting the conclusion.
3. Compare the language against the Standard Position and Acceptable Deviations.
4. If the language is missing, unclear, or unacceptable, apply the Preferred Revision only to the extent needed to cure the issue. Do not replace an entire clause if a narrow edit, short insertion, or comment-only recommendation will address the issue.
5. Use the Comment to Leave as a starting point for any redline comment.
6. Check every section of the NDA when applying the playbook. When applying redlines, ensure language in other sections of the NDA is consistent with that redline.
7. Before finalizing redlines, confirm the redline does not delete language that is then added back in substantially the same form.
Do not over-redline. Do not rewrite entire clauses if a narrow edit fixes the issue. Preserve acceptable language.
 
---
 
## Priority Legend
 
| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical | Deal-breaker or core protection. Always redline. Cannot sign without resolution. |
| High | Important Q2 position. Redline unless an acceptable deviation is present. |
| Medium | Important but flexible. Redline when materially off standard or creates avoidable risk. |
| Low | Boilerplate or drafting cleanup. Redline only if unusual, unclear, or risky. |

# Clause Rules
 
## 1. NDA Type / Mutuality
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
Mutual obligations applying equally to both parties and, if used, their Affiliates.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Unilateral NDA only where the facts truly support one-way disclosure.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
One-sided confidentiality obligations in a mutual exchange. Asymmetric obligations where only Q2 is materially restricted.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement governs the mutual disclosure and protection of Confidential Information between the parties. Each party may act as a Discloser or Recipient, and the obligations herein apply equally to each party with respect to Confidential Information it receives."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Both parties anticipate exchanging confidential information, so the obligations should apply equally on both sides.
 
---
 
## 2. Party Names / Addresses
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
Full legal names and principal business addresses for all parties. Q2 should be identified as:
 
"Q2 Software, Inc., a Delaware corporation, with offices at 10355 Pecan Park Blvd., Austin, TX 78729."
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Minor clerical fixes before signature if party identity is otherwise clear.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Blank party names or addresses. Wrong Q2 entity. Trade name only. Blank state of organization. Duplicate or conflicting defined names.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Q2 Software, Inc., a Delaware corporation, with offices at 10355 Pecan Park Blvd., Austin, TX 78729 ('Q2'), and [Counterparty Legal Name], a [State/Country] [entity type], with offices at [Address] ('[Short Name]')."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have completed the party details so the signing entities are clearly identified. Please confirm the corresponding details on your side.
 
---
 
## 3. Effective Date
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Effective as of the date of the last signature below.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
A clear non-retroactive calendar date that matches the signature timeline.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Retroactive effective date unless specifically intended. No effective date. Blank effective date plus signature-based construct.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement is effective as of the date of the last signature below (the 'Effective Date')."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We use a last-signature effective date so the Agreement starts when both parties have executed it.
 
---
 
## 4. Retroactive Coverage
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
NDA obligations should generally apply from the effective date unless retroactive coverage is intentional and business-approved.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Retroactive coverage for information already exchanged, if the business confirms that prior disclosures should be covered.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Retroactive coverage added by default without business need or where it could create uncertainty.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This NDA applies to Confidential Information disclosed on or after the date it is fully signed."
 
**Optional Revision if Retroactive Coverage Is Intended**
"This NDA applies to Confidential Information disclosed for the Purpose, whether disclosed before or after the date it is fully signed."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Please confirm whether the parties intend this NDA to cover information exchanged before signature.
 
---
 
## 5. Confidential Information Definition
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
Broad definition covering written, oral, electronic, visual, and demonstrated information, plus a reasonable-person standard.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Broad definition that still covers unmarked or oral information through context or other language.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Marked-information-only definition. Trade-secret-only definition. Exclusion of oral, visual, electronic, or demonstrated disclosures.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"'Confidential Information' means any non-public information disclosed by or on behalf of a party to the other party, in any form, whether written, oral, electronic, visual, or demonstrated, whether or not marked confidential, that the recipient knows or that by its nature a reasonable person should understand to be confidential or proprietary."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Confidential information is often shared orally, visually, or in demonstrations and is not always marked. A reasonable-person standard better reflects how information is exchanged in practice.
 
---
 
## 6. Confidential Information Exclusions
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Exclusions should include information that is:
 
1. Publicly available through no breach by Recipient.
2. Already rightfully possessed without confidentiality obligations.
3. Independently developed without reference to or use of Confidential Information.
4. Rightfully received from a third party without restriction, confidentiality obligation, or breach of duty.
**Acceptable Deviations**
Equivalent exclusions with reasonable documentation burden.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
No independent-development exclusion. No rightful-possession or third-party receipt exclusion. Third-party receipt exclusion that does not require lawful or unrestricted disclosure.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"The obligations of this Agreement do not apply to information that Recipient can demonstrate: (a) is or becomes publicly available through no breach of this Agreement; (b) was in Recipient's rightful possession before disclosure without an obligation of confidentiality; (c) was independently developed by or for Recipient without reference to or use of Confidential Information; or (d) is rightfully received from a third party without restriction, confidentiality obligation, or breach of any duty owed to Discloser."
 
**Comment to Leave**
These are standard exclusions that preserve confidentiality protections while ensuring information already known, independently developed, or properly received from another source is not restricted.
 
---
 
## 7. Independent Development
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Independent development must be without reference to or use of Confidential Information.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
"Without reference to or use of" or equivalent wording.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Independent-development exclusion that only says "without use" if a narrower edit can be made. Exclusion that does not clearly require independence from Confidential Information.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"independently developed by or for Recipient without reference to or use of any Confidential Information."
 
**Comment to Leave**
The "without reference to" limitation preserves the confidentiality obligations while keeping the standard independent-development carveout.
 
---
 
## 8. Purpose
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Purpose should be limited to evaluating, discussing, or pursuing a potential business relationship or transaction.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Narrow project-specific evaluation language tied to the contemplated transaction.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Broad business use, product development, benchmarking, analytics, AI/model training, competitive analysis, or third-party benefit.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"The parties wish to exchange Confidential Information solely for the purpose of evaluating, discussing, and pursuing a potential business relationship between them (the 'Purpose')."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Tying use of Confidential Information to a defined Purpose ensures the information is used only for the contemplated evaluation and not for unrelated commercial activities.
 
---
 
## 9. Use Restriction
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
Recipient may use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and for no other purpose.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Use only as reasonably necessary to perform or evaluate the Purpose.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
No express use restriction. Use rights broader than the Purpose.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Recipient shall use Confidential Information solely for the Purpose and for no other purpose, and shall not use Confidential Information for its own benefit or the benefit of any third party."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have aligned the use restriction with the defined Purpose so the scope of permitted use is clear.
 
---
 
## 10. Standard of Care
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Recipient must protect Confidential Information using the same degree of care it uses for its own similar confidential information, and in no event less than reasonable care.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
"At least reasonable care." "All reasonable care" is acceptable and should not be held up, though "reasonable care" is cleaner.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
No objective care standard. Purely subjective standard. Best-efforts standard without a reasonable-care floor.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Recipient shall protect Confidential Information using the same degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of a similar nature, and in any event no less than reasonable care."
 
**Comment to Leave**
An objective reasonable-care floor gives both parties a clear benchmark for safeguarding confidential information.
 
---
 
## 11. Need-to-Know Disclosure
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
Disclosure only to representatives with a need to know for the Purpose and who are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as the NDA.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Disclosure to employees, contractors, consultants, advisors, professional advisors, or representatives if they need to know for the Purpose and are subject to confidentiality obligations. Affiliate disclosure is acceptable only if Affiliate is defined or the scope is otherwise clear.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Free disclosure to affiliates, partners, contractors, or third parties without safeguards. "Representatives" used but not defined. Recipient not responsible for representative compliance.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Recipient shall (a) restrict disclosure of Confidential Information to only those employees (including employees of any wholly-owned subsidiary, a parent company, and any other wholly owned subsidiaries of the same parent company), agents or consultants of Recipient who must be directly involved with the Confidential Information for the Purpose as set forth herein, and who are bound by confidentiality terms substantially similar to those in this Agreement which enable Recipient to enforce the provisions of this Agreement. Recipient is responsible for any breach of this Agreement by its representatives."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Limiting disclosure to need-to-know representatives under confidentiality obligations is a standard safeguard that protects both parties.
 
**Surgical Redline Guidance**
Do not replace the full representative-disclosure clause if the existing clause already includes need-to-know access and confidentiality obligations. Add only the missing category or safeguard.
 
Do not add “affiliates” unless Affiliate is already defined or a definition is also being added. If affiliate disclosure is not necessary, avoid adding it.
 
If the draft already permits disclosure to employees or consultants with need-to-know and confidentiality obligations, a preferred surgical add-on is:
 
"Recipient is responsible for any breach of this Agreement by its representatives."
---
 
## 12. Affiliates / Related Bodies Corporate
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
If "Affiliate," "Affiliates," or similar corporate-group terms are used, the term must be clearly defined.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
A statutory definition, such as "related body corporate" under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth), is acceptable if appropriate for the governing law and transaction.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Use of affiliate(s) without definition. Affiliate rights or obligations broader than intended.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"'Affiliate' means, with respect to a party, any entity that directly or indirectly controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with that party, where 'control' means ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities or equivalent voting interests."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have added a definition so the scope of affiliated entities is clear.
 
---
 
## 13. Compelled Disclosure
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Prompt prior written notice unless legally prohibited, plus reasonable cooperation for protective relief.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Notice "to the extent legally permitted" and reasonable cooperation.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
No notice requirement. Requirement to obtain or attempt to obtain the disclosing party's consent before complying with legal process.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"If Recipient is required by law, regulation, subpoena, court order, regulator, stock exchange rule, or other legal process to disclose Confidential Information, Recipient shall, to the extent legally permitted, give Discloser prompt prior written notice and reasonably cooperate with Discloser in seeking a protective order or other appropriate relief."
 
**Comment to Leave**
A notice-and-cooperation construct gives the disclosing party an opportunity to seek protective relief while allowing the recipient to comply with applicable law.
 
---
 
## 14. Unauthorized Use / Disclosure Notice
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Prompt notice of known unauthorized use, disclosure, or loss of Confidential Information, plus reasonable cooperation to mitigate or remedy.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Notice obligation without detailed remediation language.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
No obligation to notify the disclosing party of known unauthorized use, disclosure, or loss.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Recipient shall promptly notify Discloser in writing upon becoming aware of any unauthorized use, disclosure, or loss of Confidential Information and shall reasonably cooperate with Discloser to mitigate or remedy such unauthorized use, disclosure, or loss."
 
**Comment to Leave**
A prompt-notice obligation allows the affected party to take reasonable steps to limit harm.
 
**Surgical Redline Guidance**
If the NDA lacks this obligation, add a single sentence to the existing confidentiality obligations section. Do not rewrite the entire confidentiality section.
 
Preferred short insert:
"Each party shall promptly notify the other party of any known unauthorized use or disclosure of the other party’s Confidential Information and reasonably assist the other party in mitigating the effects of such unauthorized use or disclosure."
---
 
## 15. Ownership / No License
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Discloser retains all right, title, and interest in its Confidential Information. No express or implied license is granted.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Clear no-transfer or no-license concept stated generally.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
IP assignment. Implied license. Use rights beyond the Purpose. Ownership creep.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"As between the parties, all Confidential Information remains the property of Discloser or its licensors. Nothing in this Agreement grants Recipient any right, title, interest, or license, whether express or implied, in or to any Confidential Information or any intellectual property rights of Discloser."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have clarified that no ownership or license rights are transferred under the NDA.
 
---
 
## 16. Warranty Disclaimer / "As Is" Language
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
Confidential Information should be provided "as is," with no express, implied, or statutory warranties. The NDA should not include representations that the disclosing party has full right, title, authority, accuracy, completeness, non-infringement, or fitness-related assurances regarding Confidential Information.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
A simple "as is" disclaimer. General ownership language that does not create a warranty.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Language stating that a party "represents," "warrants," or "confirms" it has all rights, title, authority, accuracy, completeness, permission, or ability to disclose Confidential Information.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"All Confidential Information is provided 'as is,' and each party disclaims all warranties, whether express, implied, or statutory, with respect to the Confidential Information."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Confidential Information may be preliminary or incomplete at this stage, so the NDA should not create warranty exposure for either party.
 
---
 
## 17. Term
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Two-year term from the effective date, terminable on 90 days' prior written notice, or superseded by a later agreement governing confidentiality.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
One- to two-year term. Longer term with clear endpoint or business reason.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Indefinite agreement term with no endpoint. Term tied to "the relationship" without a defined trigger.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement shall commence on the Effective Date and continue for two (2) years thereafter, unless earlier terminated by either party upon ninety (90) days' prior written notice or superseded by a subsequent written agreement between the parties governing confidentiality."
 
**Comment to Leave**
A defined term gives both parties a clear window for the contemplated discussions while allowing a later agreement to govern any ongoing relationship.
 
---
 
## 18. Term Drafting Clarity
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
Term clauses should list expiration and termination triggers clearly.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Minor drafting awkwardness that does not change meaning.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Broken "earlier of" formulation, duplicate "earlier," or unclear trigger language.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This NDA will continue until the earlier of: (a) two (2) years from the date it is fully signed; (b) the date the Purpose is achieved and the parties enter into a further agreement governing confidentiality; or (c) termination by either party on ninety (90) days' prior written notice to the other party."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Revised for clarity only.
 
---
 
## 19. Survival
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
Confidentiality and use obligations survive for five years after expiration or termination, with trade secrets protected for so long as they remain trade secrets under applicable law.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Two- to five-year survival. Perpetual survival for trade secrets only.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Indefinite survival for all Confidential Information. No survival. Survival measured from each disclosure if it creates administrative burden.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"The confidentiality and use obligations under this Agreement shall survive expiration or termination for five (5) years; provided that, with respect to information that constitutes a trade secret under applicable law, such obligations shall continue for so long as the information remains a trade secret."
 
**Comment to Leave**
A five-year post-termination survival period is administrable and preserves ongoing protection for trade secrets.
 
---
 
## 20. Return / Destruction
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
Upon written request, return or destroy Confidential Information within five to ten business days. Certification only if requested.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Reasonable stated period, such as 10–30 days. Recipient choice of return or destruction.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Immediate return or destruction. No timeframe. Technically infeasible purge obligation.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Upon Discloser's written request, Recipient shall, within ten (10) business days after such request, return or destroy all Confidential Information in its possession or control."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Added a reasonable timeframe for return or deletion.
 
---
 
## 21. Backup / Archive Copies
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
Routine backup, archival, disaster-recovery, legal-hold, and records-retention copies may be retained subject to continuing confidentiality obligations.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
System-generated backup carveout with continuing confidentiality duties.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Absolute deletion of all copies, including backups. Retention with no ongoing confidentiality obligation.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Notwithstanding the foregoing, Recipient may retain copies of Confidential Information automatically generated through routine backup, archival, disaster-recovery systems, or retained pursuant to applicable law, legal hold, or bona fide records-retention policies. Any retained copies remain subject to the confidentiality and use obligations of this Agreement for so long as retained."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Modern IT systems make absolute deletion impractical. This carveout permits routine retention while keeping the information protected.
 
**Surgical Redline Guidance**
If the NDA already includes a backup or archive copy carveout, do not replace it. Add only the missing continuing-obligations sentence.
 
Preferred short insert:
"Any retained backup or archival copies remain subject to the confidentiality and use restrictions of this Agreement for so long as they are retained."
---
 
## 22. Equitable Relief
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
Either party may seek injunctive or other equitable relief for actual or threatened breach. Prefer "may seek."
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Mutual equitable-relief clause using non-mandatory wording.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
One-sided equitable relief. "Is entitled to" language if it can be softened. No-bond language if avoidable. Fee shifting embedded in remedies clause.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Each party acknowledges that a breach or threatened breach of this Agreement may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages may not be an adequate remedy. Accordingly, the non-breaching party may seek appropriate injunctive or other equitable relief, in addition to any other remedies available at law or in equity."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have used "may seek" to preserve the ability to request equitable relief without pre-deciding the issue for the court.
 
**Surgical Redline Guidance**
Do not rewrite the entire remedies clause if targeted edits work. Replace only mandatory entitlement language such as “is entitled to” or “will be entitled to” with “may seek.” Remove “will not oppose” language if present.
---
 
## 23. Indemnification
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
No indemnification provisions in a basic mutual NDA.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Delete indemnity if present.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Indemnification for NDA breach. Defense or hold-harmless obligations layered on top of ordinary remedies.
 
**Preferred Revision**
Delete the indemnification provision.
 
**Comment to Leave**
Indemnification is not customary in a mutual NDA at the pre-transaction stage. The NDA already provides an appropriate remedy framework.
 
---
 
## 24. Attorneys' Fees
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
No attorneys' fees provision preferred.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
A fully mutual prevailing-party clause may be tolerated if consistent with Q2's standard template and negotiation context.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
One-way fee shifting. Expanded enforcement-cost reimbursement. Fee shifting embedded in equitable relief, compelled disclosure, or breach notification.
 
**Preferred Revision**
Delete the attorneys' fees provision.
 
**Comment to Leave**
Fee shifting is not customary in mutual NDAs and can create unnecessary dispute exposure. We prefer to leave fee allocation to applicable law.
 
---
 
## 25. Non-Solicit / Non-Circumvent / No-Contact
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
No non-solicit, non-circumvent, non-compete, exclusivity, standstill, or client-contact restriction in a basic NDA.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
If unavoidable, narrow employee non-solicit limited to employees with direct contact and include public-solicitation, recruiter, and employee-initiated application carveouts.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Customer restrictions. Prospect restrictions. No-contact terms. Broad employee non-solicit. Non-circumvent.
 
**Preferred Revision**
Delete the provision.
 
**Comment to Leave**
An NDA should govern confidentiality only. Hiring or business-development restrictions should be addressed separately if needed.
 
---
 
## 26. Residuals / Memory
 
**Priority:** Critical
 
**Standard Position**
No residuals clause.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Only heavily narrowed general knowledge and skills language that remains subject to confidentiality and no-license obligations.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Broad residuals or unaided-memory rights allowing use of Confidential Information without restriction.
 
**Preferred Revision**
Delete the residuals provision.
 
**Comment to Leave**
The residuals provision undermines the core confidentiality and use restrictions, so we have removed it.
 
---
 
## 27. Governing Law / Forum
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
New York preferred. Acceptable alternates: Delaware, Texas, or England and Wales for international deals. Counterparty home jurisdiction may be accepted where there is a clear business reason or where changing it would create unnecessary negotiation friction.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
New York, Delaware, Texas, England and Wales, or counterparty home state where there is a business reason for accepting it.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Non-preferred jurisdiction without business reason. Choice of law and forum mismatch without justification. Mandatory arbitration or unusual dispute procedures without review.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of New York, without regard to conflict-of-laws principles."
 
For venue (only if the draft already contains a venue clause or the user requests one):
"Each party irrevocably consents to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in New York County, New York."
 
**Fallback Revision for Jurisdiction Consistency**
"This Agreement is governed by the laws of [Jurisdiction]. The parties submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of [same Jurisdiction]."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Aligned governing law and forum for consistency.
 
**Governing Law Redline Guidance**
Do not automatically redline governing law solely because it is not New York. Redline or comment only if the jurisdiction is outside New York, Delaware, Texas, or England and Wales, or if the user asks for strict Q2-preferred redlines. For counterparty home-state law (e.g., California, Washington), use a `comment_only` optional redline rather than a mandatory replace, unless the user has requested strict redlines. Do not add a venue clause if the draft does not already have one.
 
If the governing law is the counterparty’s home-state law and there is no unusual venue, arbitration, fee-shifting, or dispute procedure, treat the issue as optional/comment-only in Business-Friendly / Surgical mode.

## 28. Assignment
 
**Priority:** Low / Medium
 
**Standard Position**
No assignment without prior written consent, with optional customary M&A successor carveout if appropriate.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Consent-required assignment provision. Binding successors and permitted assigns.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Free assignment to third parties. Assignment that conflicts with confidentiality obligations.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Neither party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other party, except to a successor in connection with a merger, acquisition, corporate reorganization, or sale of all or substantially all of its assets."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have clarified assignment to prevent transfer to unrelated third parties while preserving customary successor flexibility.
 
---
 
## 29. Existing Agreement / Supersession Carveout
 
**Priority:** High
 
**Standard Position**
The NDA should not supersede or replace existing confidentiality or non-disclosure obligations under any prior or existing agreement unless clearly intended and narrowly limited.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Generic carveout preserving existing agreements. Agreement-specific reference only when the referenced agreement actually exists and is relevant.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Broad supersession language that could override an MSA, DPA, SOW, marketplace/developer agreement, customer agreement, or other existing confidentiality regime.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This NDA does not supersede or replace any existing confidentiality or non-disclosure obligations between the parties under any prior or existing agreement, and to the extent of any conflict between this NDA and such existing agreement with respect to information exchanged under that agreement, the terms of the existing agreement will prevail."
 
**Comment to Leave**
We have preserved existing confidentiality obligations to avoid unintentionally overriding prior agreements.
 
---
 
## 30. No Obligation to Proceed
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
The NDA should state that neither party is obligated to enter into a transaction, business relationship, or further agreement.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
"No commitment until definitive agreement" language.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Language implying commitment to proceed, continue negotiations, close a transaction, or disclose specific information.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"Nothing in this Agreement obligates either party to proceed with any business relationship, transaction, or further agreement, or to disclose any particular Confidential Information."
 
**Comment to Leave**
This clarifies that the NDA supports evaluation only and does not commit either party to a transaction.
 
---
 
## 31. Modification / Amendment
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
Amendments only by written agreement signed by authorized representatives of both parties.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Standard written-amendment requirement.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Unilateral amendment. Amendment by informal conduct, email alone, or click-through.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement may be amended only by a written instrument signed by an authorized representative of each party."
 
**Comment to Leave**
A signed written amendment requirement protects both parties from unintended changes.
 
---
 
## 32. Waiver
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
No waiver unless in signed writing. One waiver does not waive future breaches.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Equivalent written-waiver language.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Implied waiver by conduct. Course-of-dealing waiver language.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"No waiver of any provision of this Agreement is effective unless in writing and signed by the waiving party. No waiver of any breach constitutes a waiver of any other or subsequent breach."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Standard waiver language ensures informal forbearance is not treated as a permanent waiver.
 
---
 
## 33. Severability
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
Invalidity of one provision does not affect the remainder.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Standard severability clause.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
No severability where the agreement is heavily customized or unstable.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, the remaining provisions remain in full force and effect."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Standard severability protects the rest of the Agreement if one provision is later found unenforceable.
 
---
 
## 34. Counterparts / Electronic Signature
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
Agreement may be executed in counterparts and by electronic signature.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Counterparts only if e-signature is not contested.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Wet-ink-only execution requirement without business reason.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement may be executed in counterparts, each of which is deemed an original and all of which together constitute one instrument. Signatures delivered electronically, including by PDF or electronic signature platform, are deemed originals."
 
**Comment to Leave**
Electronic signature is our standard execution method.
 
---
 
## 35. No Third-Party Beneficiaries
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
No third-party beneficiaries unless expressly intended.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Standard no-third-party-beneficiary clause. Limited affiliate carveout where affiliates are intended beneficiaries.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Broad third-party beneficiary rights benefiting non-parties in a basic NDA.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement is for the sole benefit of the parties and their permitted successors and assigns, and nothing in this Agreement confers any right, benefit, or remedy on any third party."
 
**Comment to Leave**
This confirms the Agreement is enforceable only by the parties unless expressly stated otherwise.
 
---
 
## 36. Successors / Permitted Assigns
 
**Priority:** Low
 
**Standard Position**
Agreement binds successors and permitted assigns, consistent with assignment restrictions.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Standard binding-effect clause.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Binding-effect language that conflicts with assignment limits or expands rights unexpectedly.
 
**Preferred Revision**
"This Agreement binds and benefits the parties and their respective successors and permitted assigns."
 
**Comment to Leave**
This keeps the binding-effect language consistent with the assignment provision.
 
---
 
## 37. Signature Block Completeness
 
**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**
Party names, signatory names, titles, and date fields should be complete and consistent with the effective-date construct.
 
**Acceptable Deviations**
Blank fields intentionally left for DocuSign or execution routing.
 
**Unacceptable Deviations**
Blank signatory/date fields in final ready-to-sign copy where effective date depends on signature timing. Signature block party name inconsistent with preamble.
 
**Preferred Revision**

**Priority:** Medium
 
**Standard Position**

**Acceptable Deviations**

In a multi-party NDA, each party's obligations should apply symmetrically to information received from any other party.

# Fast Review Flags
 
## Usually Acceptable
 
- Mutual NDA.
- Clear party names and addresses.
- Signature-based effective date.
- Broad Confidential Information definition with reasonable-person standard.
- Purpose-limited use.
- Reasonable-care standard.
- Need-to-know disclosure with confidentiality obligations.
- Compelled disclosure notice and cooperation.
- Ownership/no-license language.
- Defined term and survival.
- Return/destruction with backup carveout.
- Equitable relief using "may seek."
- No obligation to proceed.
- Existing agreement carveout.
## Usually Redline
 
- No reasonable-person standard.
- No reasonable-care floor.
- No independent-development exclusion.
- No rightful possession / third-party receipt exclusion.
- No unauthorized disclosure notice.
- No return/destruction timeframe.
- No backup carveout.
- "Is entitled to" equitable relief.
- Undefined affiliates.
- Non-preferred or mismatched governing law/forum (outside NY, DE, TX, or England and Wales)
- Warranty-style language around Confidential Information.
- Broad supersession of existing agreements.
## Usually Not Acceptable
 
- Marked-information-only definition.
- Trade-secret-only definition.
- No use restriction.
- Broad residuals / unaided-memory rights.
- Indemnification for NDA breach.
- One-way fee shifting.
- Non-solicit, non-circumvent, non-compete, exclusivity, standstill, or no-contact terms.
- IP assignment or implied license.
- Broad business use, product development, analytics, benchmarking, or AI/model training rights.
- Broad supersession that may override an MSA, DPA, SOW, customer agreement, marketplace/developer agreement, or other existing confidentiality regime.

# Redline Principles
 
 
- **Surgical by default.** Make the minimum change necessary. Prefer phrase-level or sentence-level edits over full clause rewrites.
- Preserve acceptable counterparty wording, structure, defined terms, and clause order wherever possible.
- Do not redline language merely because it differs from Q2’s preferred form. Redline only when the current language is missing, unclear, materially non-compliant, or creates avoidable risk.
- If existing language is within an acceptable fallback, leave it as-is.
- **No delete-and-readd.** Do not delete language that will be added back in substantially the same form. If most of the language is acceptable, keep it and redline only the specific words that need to change.
- Default mode is **Business-Friendly / Surgical**. Redline Critical and High issues. Redline Medium issues only when they create real risk. Do not redline Low issues unless confusing, risky, or likely to cause execution problems.
- Insert new language only for Critical or High missing clauses. Use comment-only for Medium or Low missing clauses unless the omission creates material risk.
- Do not automatically redline counterparty home-state governing law. See Section 27.
- Do not add “affiliates” unless Affiliate is defined or a definition is being added.
- Do not invent section numbers, defined terms, dates, parties, or cross-references.
- Do not cite internal playbooks or templates in counterparty-facing comments.
- Keep comments short, practical, and counterparty-facing. One sentence is the default.
- Prefer “may seek” for equitable relief.
- Always flag undefined “affiliate(s).”
- Before finalizing, review each redline in context. Do not create overlapping replacements that duplicate words, remove spaces, or stack old and new language together. If a clean phrase-level edit cannot be made reliably, use comment-only instead.
